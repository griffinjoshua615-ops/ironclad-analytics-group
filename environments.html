Ground truth from people who lived the job.

Expert-authored tasks and full environments in tacit sustainment domains, built under a protocol that frontier models have already failed. Shared or exclusive. Delivered with the probe log.

What you get

  • Tasks. Single-crux problems with one exact answer, a verifier, and a written reason the crux is judgment rather than a rule. Priced per task.
  • Environments. A working setting (files, exports, records, tools) plus a suite of tasks and verifiers, so an agent can be trained and scored end to end. Priced per environment.
  • Exclusivity. Shared items are sold to more than one buyer. Exclusive items are yours alone, at a multiple of the shared rate.
  • The probe log. Every item ships with the record of which blind solvers were run and what they returned. You see the gap before you pay for it.

Domains

  • Class IX parts judgment: dead, duplicate and superseded requisitions; shop stock; turn-ins
  • Dispatch and 5988-E fault flow
  • Deadline and NMC recovery under parts and funds constraints
  • Forward maintenance and expeditionary supply
  • Warehouse and sort operations: staffing, flow, bottlenecks
  • Month-end accrual and reconciliation with written policy (the control case)

New domains are added through a bench of veteran and industrial co-authors who work under the same protocol. Ask about a domain that is not listed.

Why the protocol matters to your rejection rate

1

Ambiguity is not difficulty. A task fails if two experts could reasonably give different answers. Each Dokimos task has one.

2

Rules are not judgment. If a blind solver clears the task on written policy alone, it is sent back. The library holds only what the models could not do.

3

Exact grading or nothing. Verifiers are deterministic. No rubric drift, no grader opinion, no argument after delivery.

Proof point: on an independent benchmark program in late 2026, the founder's Class IX judgment task went zero for five against frontier models while his written-rule accrual task was solved in every trial. Same author, same protocol. The protocol is what separates the two.
